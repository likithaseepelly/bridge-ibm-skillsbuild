# Heart failure, discharge guidance for the recovery window

Source note: compiled from publicly available patient education guidance on heart
failure self care after a hospital stay. Used in this prototype as retrieval
material only. Not clinical advice.

## Warning signs to watch in the first 30 days

- Weight gain of two to three pounds in one day, or five pounds in one week.
  Sudden weight gain usually means fluid is building up.
- Increasing shortness of breath, especially breathlessness when lying flat or
  waking at night short of breath.
- New or worsening swelling in the ankles, feet, legs or abdomen.
- A persistent dry hacking cough, or coughing that worsens when lying down.
- Dizziness, light headedness or fainting.
- Chest pain or pressure. This is an emergency sign.
- Confusion or a sudden change in alertness. This is an emergency sign.

## Plan steps that commonly fail after discharge

- Diuretic prescriptions that are never collected from the pharmacy.
- Daily weight monitoring that is never started because no scale is available.
- Follow up appointments that are scheduled but not attended.
- Sodium and fluid restriction instructions that were explained once, at the
  bedside, and not retained.

## Expected timings used by this prototype

- New prescriptions collected within 2 days of discharge.
- Follow up appointment booked within 7 days of discharge.
- Follow up appointment attended within 14 days of discharge.
