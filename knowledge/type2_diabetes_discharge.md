# Type 2 diabetes, discharge guidance for the recovery window

Source note: compiled from publicly available patient education guidance on type
2 diabetes self care after a hospital stay. Used in this prototype as retrieval
material only. Not clinical advice.

## Warning signs to watch in the first 30 days

- Blood sugar readings that are consistently above the target range set by the
  clinical team, or a single reading above 20 mmol/L.
- Blood sugar readings that are very low, with shakiness, sweating, dizziness or
  confusion. This is an emergency sign.
- Feeling much more thirsty than usual or passing much more urine than normal.
- Nausea, vomiting or stomach pain, especially alongside a high blood sugar
  reading. These can be signs of diabetic ketoacidosis and are an emergency sign.
- A wound or foot sore that is not healing, or that shows signs of infection such
  as redness, warmth, swelling or discharge.
- Blurred vision that is new or getting worse.
- Feeling unusually tired or weak for more than a day or two after discharge.

## Plan steps that commonly fail after discharge

- Glucose monitoring equipment that is prescribed but never set up or used.
- Insulin or oral medication doses that are adjusted incorrectly at home.
- Foot inspection that was advised once in hospital and never continued at home.
- Follow up blood tests, such as HbA1c checks, that are not scheduled or attended.

## Expected timings used by this prototype

- New prescriptions collected within 2 days of discharge.
- Follow up appointment booked within 7 days of discharge.
- Follow up appointment attended within 14 days of discharge.
